\section{A finite calibration corpus}

Consider the uniform three-question bipartite XOR games. Write the winning
parity at question pair $(i,j)$ as $F_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$. Each of the $512$
matrices $F$ represents a different labelled verifier. Local answer flips add
a row bit and a column bit modulo two. For any fixed ordering of questions
and parties, the least row-major mask in the answer-flip orbit has first row
and first column zero. Indeed, column flips can zero the first row, then each
subsequent row flip can zero its leading entry. These conditions determine the
resulting table uniquely:
\[
 T_{ij}=F_{ij}\mathbin{\oplus} F_{i0}\mathbin{\oplus} F_{0j}
                  \mathbin{\oplus} F_{00}.
\]
Here $F$ already has the chosen question and party ordering. Minimizing the
result over $2(3!)^2=72$ orderings gives the least mask under all the declared
relabellings. Recording the maps yields a transporter to that representative.

The table gives the witnesses used in the portable calibration corpus. The
local values have exact enumeration certificates. For the last two rows the
quantum scores come from rational projective measurements on a two-qubit Bell
state. Transport and independent checking prove a strict quantum advantage
for all $480$ labelled members of those two orbits. The remaining $32$ admit
perfect local play and an upper certificate of one.

For the third representative a different witness attains the exact optimum
$5/6$. Take four pairwise anticommuting Hermitian involutions given by the Pauli
strings $G_0=X\otimes I$, $G_1=Y\otimes I$, $G_2=Z\otimes X$, and
$G_3=Z\otimes Y$. Write $H=G_1+G_2+G_3$. Alice measures the observables
$G_0,(G_0+H)/2,(G_0-H)/2$ and Bob uses their transposes on a maximally
entangled state of local dimension four. Anticommutation makes each observable
an involution. The Bell-state correlator is the normalized trace of the
two observables' product after transposing Bob's operator. In row-major
order the nine correlators are
\[
 1,\ 1/2,\ 1/2,\quad 1/2,\ 1,\ -1/2,\quad 1/2,\ -1/2,\ 1.
\]
Their signed sum is six, giving score $1/2+6/18=5/6$. In the score convention
of Section~\ref{sec:xorcertificates}, an exact feasible XOR dual with all six
diagonal entries equal to $1/18$ gives the same upper bound.
